Idea: after each interaction, the photon’s next direction is essentially random. It makes very little outward progress on average, so the number of steps needed grows roughly with the square of distance.

For a simple random walk: N ≈ (R / l)², where R is the distance to escape and l is the mean free path.

In this demo, l is deliberately exaggerated. If l = 0.025 R☉, then N ≈ 1600. In the real Sun, the effective mean free path is tiny compared with the solar radius, so the number of interactions becomes enormous.

Use this as a qualitative model of diffusion, not as a precise solar-interior calculation.
