You said:

I want to build an interactive eclipse simulator. Ideally, this would show the inclined orbit of the moon around Earth with 3D shadows being cast by both bodies.

ChatGPT said:

Yes. This would work well as a Three.js browser simulation, with the Sun, Earth and Moon rendered in 3D and the eclipse geometry calculated analytically.

The important design decision is that the visible shadows should not rely solely on Three.js shadow mapping. A DirectionalLight is suitable for illuminating the spheres, but it treats sunlight as parallel rays and produces only a hard-edged surface shadow. It does not naturally show the converging umbra and expanding penumbra. Three.js+1

Instead, I would construct the umbra and penumbra as translucent 3D volumes.

Suggested layout

Main 3D view

The scene would contain:

  • Sun, Earth and Moon

  • the Moon’s orbital path, inclined by about 5.1∘5.1^\circ5.1∘ to the ecliptic

  • Earth’s orbital plane as a faint reference grid

  • ascending and descending nodes

  • the line of nodes

  • translucent umbral and penumbral shadow volumes

  • optional labels and distance markers

The Moon’s orbit could be dragged or animated, while the camera could be rotated, zoomed and panned using OrbitControls. Three.js

Shadow geometry

Suppose an eclipsing body has radius RbR_bRb​, the Sun has radius RsR_sRs​, and their centre-to-centre distance is DDD.

At a distance xxx behind the eclipsing body, the radius of the umbra is

rumbra(x)=Rb−Rs−RbDx.r_{\mathrm{umbra}}(x) = R_b-\frac{R_s-R_b}{D}x.rumbra​(x)=Rb​−DRs​−Rb​​x.

The umbra reaches its tip at

Lumbra=DRbRs−Rb.L_{\mathrm{umbra}} = \frac{D R_b}{R_s-R_b}.Lumbra​=Rs​−Rb​DRb​​.

The outer radius of the penumbra is

rpenumbra(x)=Rb+Rs+RbDx.r_{\mathrm{penumbra}}(x) = R_b+\frac{R_s+R_b}{D}x.rpenumbra​(x)=Rb​+DRs​+Rb​​x.

These can be represented using translucent cone or frustum meshes aligned with the direction pointing directly away from the Sun.

For a solar eclipse, the Moon supplies RbR_bRb​. For a lunar eclipse, Earth supplies RbR_bRb​. NASA describes these shadows as a smaller central umbra, in which the Sun is completely blocked, surrounded by the penumbra, in which it is only partially blocked. NASA Science+1

Why eclipses do not happen every month

The simulator should make the lunar inclination prominent. The Moon’s orbit is inclined by about 5.1∘5.1^\circ5.1∘ to the ecliptic and intersects it at two nodes. Most new and full moons therefore pass above or below the necessary alignment. NASA Eclipse

A useful visual would be:

  • new Moon near a node: solar eclipse

  • full Moon near a node: lunar eclipse

  • new or full Moon away from a node: shadow misses the target

That would make the purpose of the nodes immediately visible.

Useful camera presets

System view
Shows the inclined orbit and both orbital planes.

Along the line of nodes
Makes the lunar inclination especially clear.

View from the Sun
Shows whether the Sun, Moon and Earth overlap.

View from Earth
Shows the apparent discs of the Sun and Moon during a solar eclipse.

Shadow view
Looks down the shadow axis, showing the target body moving through the umbra and penumbra.

Surface effects

The 3D shadow volumes would explain the geometry, but the target bodies should also react visually.

For a solar eclipse:

  • find where the Moon’s shadow axis intersects Earth

  • place a marker at the centre of the eclipse

  • draw approximate umbral and penumbral footprints

  • darken the affected region on Earth

  • optionally show a small observer view of the Sun

For a lunar eclipse:

  • calculate the Moon’s distance from Earth’s shadow axis

  • gradually darken the Moon as it enters the penumbra

  • mask the portion inside the umbra

  • tint the Moon copper-red during totality

Scaling

A completely accurate scale would be difficult to use because the bodies are tiny compared with the distances between them. The simulator should therefore have two modes:

Teaching scale

  • enlarged Earth and Moon

  • compressed distances

  • exaggerated orbital inclination

  • clearly visible shadow volumes

Proportional scale

  • correct relative radii and distances

  • camera automatically follows the active body

  • shadow volumes remain selectable even when visually narrow

Any exaggerated quantity should be clearly labelled.

The best first build would be a single-file Three.js prototype with draggable lunar position, adjustable inclination, solar/lunar modes, translucent shadow cones and several camera presets. Once that geometry is working, observer views and Earth-surface eclipse tracks can be added.
